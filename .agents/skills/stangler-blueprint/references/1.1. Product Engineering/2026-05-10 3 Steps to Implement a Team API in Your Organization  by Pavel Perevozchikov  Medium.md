Cut the chaos. Speed up delivery.
“You have a structure. But do you have clarity?” — Inspired by  Team Topologies   and  The Elegant Puzzle
Cross-team dependencies often feel like navigating a maze with no map. Projects stall. Decisions drag on. Nobody’s sure who owns what.
Sound familiar?
That’s the pain of implicit communication and unclear boundaries.
A Team API helps teams become discoverable, predictable, and easy to interact with — not unlike the software APIs we use every day.
Here’s how to start.
🚀 Step 1: Define the Boundaries
Start by clarifying each team’s purpose and responsibilities:
What does our team actually do?
What systems, services, or components do we own?
What APIs, interfaces, or deliverables do we provide to others?
This aligns with the Stream-Aligned Team concept from Team Topologies — clear ownership is the foundation of flow.
🚦 Step 2: Make Collaboration Explicit
Most delays happen not because teams are unwilling, but because interaction models are implicit.
Define:
How can others reach out to us (Slack channel, Jira ticket, office hours)?
What are our typical SLA s for questions, reviews, or integration support?
How do we handle escalations?
These agreements turn coordination from ad hoc to intentional. They also help avoid the “Manager of Managers” trap described in The Elegant Puzzle.
📘 Step 3: Write it Down and Keep it Fresh
Each team should maintain a Team API document. Not a 40-page confluence page nobody reads — a concise, actionable, living artifact.
Include:
Scope & Responsibilities
Points of Contact
SLA Agreements
Key Tools / Repos / Systems
Interaction Guidelines
This isn’t bureaucracy. It’s an acceleration tool. Like good API docs for code, it makes your team easier to consume.
⚡ Final Thoughts
A well-crafted Team API reduces misalignment, improves onboarding, and creates trust between teams.
Start small, iterate, and treat it as a product.
