But in diary #2 I only briefly explained what ADR looks like. Well, in this article, I will explain in more detail what ADR is that people often use, or at least the one I use in my work. What is an Architecture Decision Record (ADR)? Before we move to Architecture Decision Record, first, we need to know what architectural decisions are. On Wikipedia, we can find that Architectural Decisions are, Architecture Decision are design decisions that address architecturally significant requirements; they are perceived as hard to make and/or costly to change. In his blog post, Documenting Architecture Decisions, Michael Nygard states that they maintain a significant collection of architectural records that can influence the structural and non-functional characteristics, dependencies, interfaces, and techniques used in system development. From these two explanations, we can understand that it is important to save the decisions that have been made when building the system, especially all architecturally significant decisions. Maybe as an example, I can mention. One day at the company Tumorang Finance as one of the biggest Digital Insurance company. Tumorang Finance wants to expand to Indonesia, so the main thing in this expansion plan is the integration with any local payment methods in Indonesia. Long story short, Tumorang Finance will integrate with a Payment Gateway, call it Xendit. Then the system was designed in such a way, until at some point, John Snow as Software Architect at Tumorang Finance argued with the engineer team regarding the choice of technology to be used. The Engineer team wanted to use RabbitMQ, then John Snow as the software architect chose to use SQS as the message queuing platform that will be used when making payment requests to Xendit (Payment Gateway). A long debate was carried out, until at a point, they found a agreeable decision and they both agreed to use RabbitMQ. From the example above, the decision to choose RabbitMQ and SQS will affect the structure and dependencies of the system to be built. If they use SQS, they automatically have to use the AWS Cloud provider. If they use RabbitMQ, they can use self-hosted or other vendors. Now the decision they choose will be stored in a record, it can be in the form of Google docs, Atlassian Confluence, Markdown on Github, etc. Now this record is called the Architecture Decision Record. With the growth of the team and the company, the people inside definitely come and go. Old people may resign, and new people will join. And this will bring an issue for new people when joining the company in the future. They may need to know, “Why was such a decision made”? Why did the previous team choose RabbitMQ over SQS? But because of the ADR, they can read and understand clearly why the decision was made. ADR Components There are many examples on the Internet of how to write ADR. There are several resources that may be useful for us to follow. But here I will try to explain again in my own explanations. ADR components If we look at the picture above, there are several important components in ADR. Even if there are many versions of ADR on the internet, these five components are the most important components when making ADR.
Title The first very important component is the Title. This will be the identifier of the ADR we are going to create. The format is free, but the most important part of this component is that it must have a number, followed by the main title of the ADR. Example: ADR 1: Using RabbitMQ over SQS or 1. Using RabbitMQ over SQS This number is useful as a reference and decision record versioning which I will explain later at the end of this article.
Status This component represents the current state of the ADR that has been written. Usually, the stages start from the “proposed” when the ADR is still waiting for approval from all stakeholders involved. Then it is changed to “accepted” when all stakeholders agree. With business growth and the company becomes bigger, new regulations or new flow introduced, the decision may be no longer relevant, then the status will be changed to “deprecated” or “ superseded by  ” if there is a new ADR that replaces the obsolete ADR.
Context This component explains the background of why  the ADR exists. Usually, this component is neutral and only explains the facts related to a problem. This component provides the existing options (example: option 1: using Golang, option 2: using JavaScript). Also would be better if we also put all the pro-cons between the options listed on this component.
Decision This component describes decisions that have been agreed upon by all stakeholders involved. Here will be explained all the reasons why this decision was taken. Which will answer all the questions “Why….”  on the architectural design that has been made. And when writing the decision, it must be written in an active voice, “We will …” stating that by consensus, all stakeholders will choose the decision in the ADR. Not “I will …” or “The CTO will …” because this is an individual decision, not a jointly agreed decision.
Consequences This component will explain all the decisions' negative effects or drawbacks/tradeoffs. So that all stakeholders are aware that the decisions that have been agreed upon have tradeoffs that must be dealt with. Then later, Tumorang Finance also managed to expand to Indonesia. Many clients register and become active members of the digital insurance offered by Tumorang Finance. Then, two years later, it turns out that the infra team is starting to be overwhelmed with maintaining their own RabbitMQ. The sparks of issues began to appear because RabbitMQ was down and became a bottleneck for the company to grow more aggressively. John Snow, an architect at Tumorang Finance, sees this as a moment to try to solve this problem. Long story short, after discussions and meetings with all stakeholders involved, it was concluded that there were two options. Use managed RabbitMQ vendors (e.g., CloudAmqp.com) Migrate to Google Pubsub So John Snow also created a new ADR with the following “proposed “ status. Then, the ADR will be discussed with all the stakeholders involved, CTO, Engineers, Infrastructure, etc. So, if the ADR is approved by all stakeholders, then the ADR status will be changed to “accepted”, while the old ADR, which was previously selected decision to choose RabbitMQ, will become “superseded”. Now, after this, the next step is to continue the design and development process and follow the agreed ADR. Conclusion In this article, I explain a little about ADR, where there are five important components: Title, Status, Context, Decision, and Consequences. These five components must be stated when making ADR. We can also be more creative in adding other components such as date or tagging (hashtags) to make ADR search/filter easier. Because this ADR must be easy to access, either by searching or by categorization and tagging. Many platforms can be used, you can use Google Docs, Atlassian Confluence, Notion, or even Markdown on Git (Github/Gitlab/Bitbucket).
